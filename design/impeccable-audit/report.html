Verdict: the rules are specific, the screens are generic

All four reviewers reached the same verdict independently. The brand is real: the ink bar, the {STEEL CITY CODES} //DENVER wordmark, gold kept for primary buttons. But it sits on a stock software kit: a cream background, white rounded cards with both a border and a shadow, uppercase icon labels over every section, icons in tinted circles, initials on every row, and status pills that repeat the heading. Cover the wordmark and the parent area could be any family portal, and the admin any membership dashboard.

The product shows through its words and its rules: the one-sentence status ("Maya is all set for camp. One spot is on a waitlist."), confirmations that name the real consequence with live counts, a roll call whose "Mark all present" never overwrites a co-volunteer's marks. What the screens never use is the product's actual shape. A family's season is campers by weeks. The database allows one class per week. Seats open at a fixed minute, a waitlist offer has a clock, and a camp day has three roll calls.

The automated detector found one textbook problem in 124 source files: a coloured side stripe on a toast. The slop here isn't the famous tells like gradients or purple glass. It is decoration by default, which only an element-by-element review can see.

What already works

  • Confirmations without modals. Cancel, switch, move, drop a duty: each confirm sits under the thing it changes, focuses the safe choice and states the consequence with live numbers.
  • Real product thinking in the roll call and recovery flows. The roll call defaults to the time of day and only shows a call button on absent rows. Password reset tells an expired link from an invalid one. The volunteer form spots an existing account and retries only the interview booking.
  • A clean colour system and a solid accessibility floor. No hard-coded colours, AA text contrast in both themes, labels on every input, 82 live regions.

The problems that matter, in order

The five assessments raised 1 P0 and 26 P1 issues, many of them the same problem seen from different roles. Merged, they come to ten. P0 blocks a task; P1 is a major difficulty or an accessibility failure.

  1. P0

    Nobody can see whether registration is open

    "Open" depends on a switch in the form editor plus an opening time in Sessions. Neither shows on the admin home, and either flips with one click and no confirm. On opening night the director's one question has no answer on screen.

    Fix: one registration status on Today ("Open since 7:00 PM · 84 campers placed · 38 seats left"), one place to change it, and a confirm before closing a live form.

  2. P1

    Safety information hides behind taps

    A volunteer sees a camper's allergy only by tapping a 24px chip that always says "Medical". The call button is an unlabelled phone icon, so the volunteer can't see who will answer. The admin list truncates "Bee stings — EpiPen in backp…". The paper roster is safer than the phone.

    Fix: health lines always visible, split "Allergy" and "Medical", with call lines naming the contact, relationship and number. Never truncate health text.

  3. P1

    The phone rules hold for volunteers and break for parents

    Inputs are 14px on signup, login, apply and every parent form, so iPhones zoom on each tap. Parent tap targets are 24–34px. Below 640px parent pages have no navigation at all. Step 3 of the volunteer application scrolls sideways.

    Fix: shared 16px fields, 44px controls, top tabs at every width.

  4. P1

    The most-used screens answer their question last

    On a phone, a parent's first camper starts about 1,700px down, below a schedule and three updates. A volunteer at 9:00 roll call sees one and a half campers on a small Android. The admin home is a greeting, five stat cards and a grid that repeats the sidebar.

    Fix: a single sign at the top with the next decision, then a list of the other decisions. Feeds and schedules fold or move to their own tab.

  5. P1

    Red is spent on good news, gold on everything

    A full section gets the same red ring as an unaccounted camper, so opening night is the reddest screen in the admin. Gold marks tabs, filters, toggles, 17 "Book" buttons and initials drawn gold on pale gold (1.44:1).

    Fix: red only for safety, failure and destructive actions. A full section is shown in plain ink. Gold only for the next action.

  6. P1

    Forms show one error at a time, at the bottom, and look filled before they are

    Placeholders look like typed answers, and the signature boxes show the expected names. Errors appear at the bottom one at a time and aren't tied to a field. Raw database errors reach parents and teenagers.

    Fix: validate the whole step, put each error under its field and focus the first one, put instructions in the labels, and write recovery copy.

  7. P1

    Some copy isn't true

    The countdown says "add your campers now", but the wizard stops at step 4 until opening. Step 4 says camp runs 8 to 4; classes run 9 to 3. Every newcomer is greeted "Welcome back". Signup with an existing email says "Check your email" and sends nothing.

    Fix: gate only the final Register button before opening, pull hours from data, use "Sign in" and "Create a parent account", and detect existing accounts.

  8. P1

    Focus rings and field edges are nearly invisible in light mode

    The gold focus ring measures 1.65:1, field borders 1.69:1, and 11 inputs drop their border when focused (WCAG 1.4.11 and 2.4.7).

    Fix: an ink focus outline (gold in dark mode), field borders at 3:1 or more, and keep the border on focus.

  9. P1

    Two different designs for one decision

    The wizard picks a class by week with radio buttons. The class browser groups by course, with admin codes, "6 / 14" fill counts, eight identical gold buttons and a 4-second toast. The yearly check retypes 13 fields, and only appears after the parent taps Register.

    Fix: one week-and-class picker for both, with the yearly check shown first as a summary.

  10. P1

    Admins manage sections, but sections hide in accordions

    A class-level fill bar hides full sections (Python 39/56 while one section is 10/10), crew appear as initials, and "Find cover" leads to a generic page and a list of 22 bare names.

    Fix: a week × class timetable showing every section's fill, waitlist and lead, with deep links from Today.

Before and after

First screen on a 390px phone, unless marked. The redesign keeps every feature, the wordmark and the brand gold, and adds no new features.

Parent home

  • The sign holds the one next decision: Noor needs a class, with the number that makes it urgent (21 spots left in the classes that fit her).
  • Maya is already set, so her room and Monday drop-off sit on her own Week 1 line.
  • Each camper shows their weeks as rows with one status each. Initials, rollup badges and icon labels are gone.
  • Schedule folded; updates moved to their own tab; contact details under the avatar.

Volunteer roll call

  • The sticky sign is one slim row: Arrival / After lunch / Dismissal and the live count.
  • Every allergy and medical note is a visible line on the camper's row.
  • An absent camper shows who to call and the number, as text.
  • Initials, ages and the "Confidential" footnote are cut.

Login and demo

  • The demo seats read like a signpost: four destinations, each with its story and an arrow.
  • "Welcome back" becomes "Sign in". A "New here?" block names the two ways in: register a camper, or apply to volunteer.
  • Inputs are 16px and the placeholders that looked filled are gone.

Admin Today

  • The sign is the P0 fix: whether registration is open, seats left, campers and waitlist, and when it last updated.
  • To-do as a list sorted by severity. Full sections are good news in plain ink, never red.
  • A week × class seat grid replaces five stat cards and the "Jump to" grid. The sidebar drops from 11 items to 5 plus Setup.

The direction: Terminal Yellow

Impeccable's direction roll assigned a "camp T-shirt" look. You chose the alternative, airport wayfinding, which won on clarity. Every screen is a junction sign: one gold band holds the one next decision, with the number you act on (room, time, count, seats) in large type, and an arrow on the edge it points past. Everything below is a list of other decisions, one per line, divided by heavy ink rules.

Each colour has one job. Gold marks the sign, the active tab and the single primary action, nothing else. Red is for safety and destructive actions. Green means done. Ink carries everything else. The background is a neutral grey instead of cream. Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, a typeface designed for legibility, carries the interface with numerals that line up in columns. Josefin Sans is kept for page titles, and the wordmark stays on ink.

Five rules came from the other worlds in the roll: a single layer with no floating cards; the same fields in the same order on every person row; schedule blocks as tall as they are long; section codes and week numbers set the same way everywhere; hierarchy from type size and weight, not from boxes.

Decisions only you can make

  • Waiver text. Is the volunteer and camper waiver SCC national's official text? If not, it is invented legal copy. The mockup marks it as a placeholder.
  • Adult volunteers. "Current grade" has no true answer for adults. Add "Not in school", or make it optional?
  • Early registration steps. Should parents fill every wizard step before opening, with only the final Register button gated? The mockup shows that, because it makes the countdown's "get ready now" true.
  • Waitlist position. Can a parent see their place in line? "#2 in line" would be the most reassuring line the waitlist could show.
